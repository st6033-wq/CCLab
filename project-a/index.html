[ Homebody ]

Scientific Name:

Luminella domestica, commonly called Homebody.

Discovered by / at:

Sally Tan, April 7, 2026.

Family:

Nestlight family: small, glowing creatures that build spiral nests and return to them after exploring.

Habitat:

A quiet underwater meadow with swaying grass, floating flowers, and slowly spinning thorn formations.

Appearance:

A glowing round body surrounded by thirty soft, beaded tentacles. Its colors slowly shift between cyan and white. Near its nest, its body grows fuller and its tentacles draw inward.

Key Characteristics:

  • Follows a gentle human guide.
  • Reacts differently to pink and yellow flowers.
  • Recoils when it touches thorns.
  • Always finds a reason to return home.

What we know so far about Homebody

Homebody is curious about the world, but most comfortable near its spiral nest. Away from home, it stretches out its tentacles and drifts through the meadow. When it returns, its tentacles curl inward, its light turns pale, and a ripple spreads from the nest.

Its favorite flowers seem to affect its mood. Large pink flowers make it glow blue and seek the comfort of home. Small yellow flowers bring a brief burst of energy: it turns golden, spins faster, and spreads its tentacles. Uneaten flowers slowly withdraw and bloom again elsewhere.

The meadow is not entirely welcoming. Spinning thorns block its path. When Homebody brushes against one, it briefly contracts and trembles, while a ripple spreads around the obstacle.

Guidelines for human interaction

Press and hold the mouse inside the scene to guide Homebody. Move toward a flower to let it feed, or lead it around the spinning thorns. Release the mouse to let it wander on its own. Try both flower types, then guide it back to the spiral nest and watch how it settles down.
